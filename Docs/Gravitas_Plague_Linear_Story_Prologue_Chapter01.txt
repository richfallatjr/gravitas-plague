Gravitas Plague - Linear Story Source

Prologue and Chapter 1

This document reconstructs the current production story in the most linear
useful order for writing. It follows the requested device order:

Prologue
  Walkie -> Battle01 -> Crank Radio -> Walkie -> Ham Receiver -> Dad Photo

Chapter 1
  Walkie -> Dad at Window / Robot -> Dad Photo -> Walkie -> Ham Receiver
  -> Dad Final Battle -> Dad Photo

Source key

- PR is fixed authored dialogue played from an MP3. Text is copied from the
  production prerecording descriptor unless otherwise noted.
- Script Voice is exact authored prose that Foundation mechanically prepares
  for generated Big Mike speech. Its wording is fixed even though it is rendered
  through TTS.
- PromptVoice Story Intent is the exact authored narrative input. Foundation
  decides the final spoken wording at runtime, so there is no single canonical
  PromptVoice transcript in the repository.
- ConversationVoice is player-dependent and has no fixed canonical dialogue.
  It uses the current point's authored context, character profile, PR context,
  and the player's current statement. It does not add durable conversation
  history to the story.
- The Dad-window and Dad-battle PR descriptors intentionally contain no text.
  Their dialogue below is a local transcription of the production MP3 and is
  labeled accordingly.

He wants the ham equipment located and quietly prepared, but he does not want

Battle01 - Mrs. Dempsey

Portal entrance

available. The player may open the door during the pre-attack timeline.

the room. She holds an idle pose for five seconds. She performs one authored
right ninety-degree turn, commits the new facing direction, then performs the
same right turn a second time. After the full one-hundred-eighty-degree turn,
she walks the authored three-dimensional route from zombie_a1 through
zombie_a2 to zombie_a3, climbing the exterior slope with her feet grounded.

If the player has already opened the door, she continues toward the threshold.
If the door is still closed when she reaches zombie_a3, she waits in idle while
the real door animation and sound open it. Player door control locks at that
last anchor. She crosses the portal using the exterior mirror and the hidden
real-world source so that one animation clock carries her smoothly into the
room.

Fight and aftermath

Mrs. Dempsey uses the established Grandma follow, attack, hit, and death
behavior. Every valid head strike layers the head-snap reaction over her current
animation. A random thirty-three percent of eligible head punches becomes an
accepted damage point and a full-body interrupt. Her Story accepted-hit capacity
is twice the current Horde value. Her attacks produce the authored combat
feedback, but Battle01 does not spend a player-death hit budget.

When her death animation finishes, the heavy Grandma runtime and portal mirror
are released and the full exterior can unload. Three to five seconds later, the
attack score crossfades into the looping cabin aftermath music at minus
twenty-five decibels. That aftermath score continues while Rich uses the four
post-battle devices.

The four devices are all gameplay-available after Battle01. For a linear story
read, the remainder of the Prologue is ordered below as Crank Radio, Walkie,
Ham Receiver, and Dad Photo.


2. Crank Radio - The Official Broadcast

The player presses Play over the crank radio. Random tuning static begins first.
Foundation prepares the PromptVoice response before the alarm and PR begin. The
data-burst alert then sounds, the emergency broadcast plays, and Broadcaster TTS
computes while the PR is audible. Low radio static remains under the alarm, PR,
generated Broadcaster speech, and any later radio conversation.

Broadcaster PR

This is an automated emergency broadcast.

Please stay in your homes. Lock all doors and windows. Do not approach individuals displaying cloudy eyes, loss of speech, abnormal posture, or unprovoked aggression.

The pathogen currently designated ATNV-15 affects rapid fear-response pathways in the mammalian brain. Infected hosts may appear confused, injured, or familiar.

Do not attempt physical contact. Do not attempt verbal de-escalation at close range. Recognition does not indicate recovery.

If a host has entered your residence, create distance. Avoid bodily fluid exposure. Remain calm. Scan your surroundings. Wait for containment instructions.

This message will repeat.

Stay indoors. Stay quiet. Stay away from the infected.

PromptVoice Story Intent

Continue the public emergency broadcast with additional verified guidance. Reinforce staying indoors, securing entrances, avoiding contact with aggressive animals or bodily fluids, monitoring this frequency, and waiting for clear verified instructions. Clearly state that no verified cause, cure, or broader operational details are available at this time. Do not address any individual listener or introduce private events, Gravitas Corporation, antigen drones, or unverified government operations.

The generated continuation remains an impersonal public broadcast. It does not
know Rich and never addresses him. It reinforces verified survival guidance and
explicitly refuses to claim a cause, cure, or hidden government operation.

ConversationVoice - Tuning for related information

The microphone over the crank radio lets the player ask about the broadcast.
The Broadcaster does not recognize a private question. The answer sounds like
the radio has tuned into a relevant continuation, clarification, or repeated
piece of public guidance. The conversation introduces no private knowledge of
Rich, Big Mike, Dad, Mrs. Dempsey, the cabin, Gravitas drones, or events inside
the house.

4. Ham Receiver - CatEye81 and the Gravitas Beacon

The player presses Play over the ham receiver. Three ScriptPoints run
consecutively: CatEye81 calls for any station, Rich answers, and CatEye81 gives
the beacon procedure. Tuning static bridges CatEye81's generated gaps. Rich's
outgoing transmission uses Rich filler and the receiver's ambient radio bed.

Ham Script01 - CatEye81 calls into the silence

PR - CatEye81

Any station monitoring, this is Cat Eye Eight-One, transmitting from Chatham County, Georgia. I haven’t heard a voice I could verify in three days. Savannah’s gone quiet, and most of what’s left on the bands is static or folks repeating rumors. I’ve been sweeping north, west, and down the coast for emergency beacons, road reports, or word of safe passage through the South. I’m sheltered for now, but power and supplies won’t last forever. If anybody copies, answer with a handle and general region only—no exact locations. Reckon I just need to know somebody else is still listening.

PromptVoice Story Intent

I haven’t spoken to another person in days, and I’m starting to believe I may be one of the last people left in this part of Georgia. I’ve been sweeping the bands for surviving stations, emergency beacons, and any word of safe passage through the South. I need to know whether the roads, waterways, or any organized route out of Chatham County are still open. My family is gone, Savannah has gone quiet, and I’m alone in the old storm shelter with food, water, batteries, and generator fuel running low. Hearing another human signal gives me hope, but I’m still guarded and I won’t give my exact location yet. I want to establish a dependable frequency, trade only information we can verify, and find out what is still alive beyond this shelter.

CatEye81 is not merely asking for information. She is trying to establish that
another human being still exists. Her supplies and power are finite, her family
is gone, and she does not know whether any organized route out of Chatham County
survives. Hope makes her transmit; caution keeps her location private.

Ham Script02 - Rich answers from Pennsylvania

PR - Rich

Cat Eye Eight-One, this is Rich. I copy you from the Pennsylvania region. My handle’s Rich—exact location stays off the air for now. I heard a report about a Gravitas beacon, something that can call an antigen drone and get doses out to isolated stations. Have you heard anything you trust? If it’s real, give me the band, the exact dial frequency, and what I’m listening for. My set’s old, so say it slow. Over.

PromptVoice Story Intent

I’m relieved to hear another living person answer, but I’m trying not to sound too eager or give away more than I should over an open frequency. I’m somewhere in Pennsylvania, and for now I’m only giving my handle and general region. I’ve heard fragments about a Gravitas beacon that can request an antigen delivery by drone, but I have never heard the beacon myself and I do not know whether the reports are real.

I need CatEye81 to tell me what she personally heard, what came from another operator, and whether anyone actually confirmed receiving an antigen package. I need the band, exact dial frequency, sideband mode, transmission schedule, and the identifying sound or code that separates the real Gravitas relay from interference or somebody imitating it. My ham equipment is old and only partly functional, so I need the numbers spoken slowly and repeated clearly. I may also need to know what information the relay expects, whether a handle and grid square are enough, and how long I have to remain on frequency after making contact.

I’m trying to sound technical and skeptical because that is easier than admitting how badly I need this to be real. A working Gravitas relay would be the first concrete evidence that somebody outside the immediate area is still organized and capable of helping. I also do not want this contact with CatEye81 to disappear after one exchange. I want to establish a dependable frequency and time to call again, compare what each of us is hearing, and trade only information we can verify.

Rich hides his hope behind technical questions. He wants the frequency, mode,
schedule, identifier, authentication procedure, and evidence of a completed
delivery. He also wants something more durable than one lucky contact: a person
he can reach again.

Ham Script03 - CatEye81 gives the beacon procedure

PR - CatEye81

Rich, Cat Eye Eight-One. Copy Pennsylvania. Yeah—the Gravitas relay is real. I’ve heard the identifier myself, and I copied one station confirming an antigen drop. Go to sixty meters, Channel Two. Set your dial to five point three four six five megahertz, upper sideband. That’s 5.3465 USB. Listen for three clipped data bursts, about a minute apart. Don’t key over the first pass. Once the identifier repeats clean, move to Channel One—5.3305 USB—and check in with your handle and six-character grid square, not a street address. They’ll confirm the request there. If they accept it, Gravitas sends the antigen package by drone and homes it on your carrier. You copy?

PromptVoice Story Intent

I’m relieved the Pennsylvania station asked about the Gravitas beacon because I’ve been holding onto information I wasn’t sure I would ever get to share. Three nights ago, I copied the same automated identifier twice on the sixty-meter band. Later, I heard another operator report that Gravitas accepted their request and delivered a sealed antigen package by drone. I did not witness the delivery myself, but I heard enough of the exchange to believe the relay is real.

The automated beacon is on 5.3465 megahertz upper sideband, sixty-meter Channel Two. It transmits three short digital bursts and repeats the same identifier near the top and bottom of the hour. After the identifier is copied cleanly, the operator moves to 5.3305 megahertz upper sideband, Channel One, and checks in with a handle and six-character Maidenhead grid square. The grid gives Gravitas a general delivery area without broadcasting a street address.

A legitimate relay repeats the station’s handle and returns a short confirmation code when the request is accepted. The operator should write that code down, remain near the receiver, and keep monitoring the voice channel because Gravitas may request another check-in before dispatching the drone. I have also heard imitation signals on nearby frequencies, so the repeated identifier and confirmation code matter. The real relay does not ask for a full name or exact address.

I want to give the Pennsylvania station every detail I copied, but I need to distinguish what I heard personally from what another operator reported. I’m hopeful this could keep somebody alive, and I’m relieved to finally have useful information instead of another rumor. I should speak slowly when giving the frequencies, ask for them to be repeated back, and ask the other station to tell me whether the identifier matches the one I heard.

CatEye81 distinguishes direct evidence from secondhand radio reports. The
procedure is now concrete: identify the legitimate three-burst signal on
5.3465 USB, move to 5.3305 USB, identify only by handle and six-character grid,
record the returned confirmation code, and remain near the receiver. Spoof
signals exist, so repetition and the confirmation code matter.

2. Dad Photo - Four Chances

After the Robot encounter and its full runtime cleanup, the Dad Photo is the
first post-Robot device to unlock.

PR - Rich

That picture was twenty years ago. Dad looks like he knew exactly where he belonged. I keep looking at his face in it, then thinking about the face I saw at that window. He didn’t wander past the house. He came right to the glass and stopped. For a second, I swear he knew me. Now I’ve got four antigen cartridges sitting on that cart. Four chances. I don’t know how long he’s been sick, or whether early stage is already behind him, but I can’t decide he’s gone. Not after he found his way home.

PromptVoice Story Intent

I am forcing myself to separate what I actually witnessed from what I desperately want to believe. Dad followed the same route he used after late engineering shifts, coming past the side window before walking around to the front door. When I called to him, he stopped and turned toward my voice. His hand moved toward the lower window frame near the loose seal he used to complain about fixing. That may have been recognition, an old habit, or merely the infection reacting to sound, but it is the first evidence I have that some part of him may still be present.

The Gravitas pack contains four individually sealed antigen cartridges. I have already started thinking of one as Dad’s, even though I do not know the treatment window, the proper way to administer it, or whether he is still early-stage. One cartridge should probably be held back for myself. The remaining two suddenly feel like moral decisions instead of supplies. Every person I encounter may become a choice about who deserves another chance.

The Robot described the antigen as temporary suppression, not a cure. I need to learn whether recognition of home, familiar voices, repeated habits, or purposeful movement means the treatment could still work. I also need a way to bring Dad close without opening the house to him or anything that may be following him.

Dad taught me never to declare a machine dead until I had traced every connection. People are not machines, but I am holding onto that lesson. I want to use the photograph, his old tools, the workshop light, and familiar sounds to test whether memory can still reach him before I risk a cartridge—or my life.

Rich turns four cartridges into four moral choices. He recognizes the danger of
mistaking habit for consciousness, but Dad's familiar route and movement toward
the broken window seal feel too specific to dismiss. He decides not to call Dad
gone until he has tested every remaining connection.
